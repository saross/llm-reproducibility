1. Instrument clarifications (FAIR instrument v2.0 to v2.1)

The canonical instrument gains the following operative sections (ratified erratum-log Entry 3, items 1–7 and 9; full text in the canonical file at the tagged repository commit, mirrored verbatim in the Pass 6 prompt and registered with a content-integrity hash). First, the research-surface rule: the unit of assessment is the paper's research surface — the complete set of digital artefacts (data, code, and other digital inputs) required to reproduce the paper's reported results, as reachable from the published paper. Each FAIR sub-principle scores the empirical status of those artefacts; creator identity, depositor identity, and responsibility for closure never affect scores. A precisely cited, well-archived third-party input earns full credit; a closed or unpublished input is penalised even where closure is beyond the authors' control. Provenance (author-deposited, third-party, or undeterminable) is recorded per required input as non-scoring metadata. The rationale cites the verified precedent set (RDA FAIR Data Maturity Model v1.00, 10.15497/rda00050; the Journal of Industrial Ecology Data Openness Badges secondary-data rule, 10.1111/jiec.12738; CODECHECK; Colavizza et al. 2020; Culina et al. 2020; Tedersoo et al. 2021; Marwick 2017); ACM Artifact Review and Badging v1.1 is the named deliberate departure — the study measures the credibility of published results, not author compliance.

Second, an initial and explicitly iterable aggregation rule: within each artefact type, sub-principles score the principal artefact(s) — those whose absence would block reproduction — conjunctively for data; the paper's own analysis scripts are always principal for code, and third-party dependencies enter only through citation quality (I3) and the evidence pack. Alternatives (majority-over-principals; proportion-weighted) are recorded for iteration.

Third, an evidence-admissibility ladder with a platform entitlement table. Admissible evidence has two rungs: rung (i), direct evidence — the paper's text and the verified per-paper evidence pack (section 2); rung (ii), by-construction inference from a closed, instrument-listed platform table, applicable only where rung (i) is silent. Entitlements are floors, not ceilings, and the registered unscoreable-scores-0 default applies only after the ladder is exhausted. The table's rows were verified at the granting services on 2026-08-15 (dated verification note and registry enrichment in the repository), and the v2.1 table incorporates the corrections that verification forced. The DataCite row: the DOI string is undeletable and Handle-resolvable, but public metadata accessibility is not guaranteed after withdrawal (DataCite's own retraction workflow contradicts the tombstone claim); mandatory metadata is satisfiable with null-codes; DataCite mandates no licence property. The Zenodo row: the licence-field guarantee is scoped to open-access records; verified persistence entitlements are added (tombstone page, DOI retained, metadata always accessible — platform policy assertions, third-party-unaudited); a default-licence footnote records that 82.6% of sampled records carry the pre-filled CC-BY-4.0 default, and a default-valued licence is fully operative — the instrument assesses the research surface, not intent, and default-domination is interpretive context, never a score modifier. The CRAN row: the strongest metadata floor in the table and a licence field with no default value, but no persistent identifier, and archival is a norm rather than a guarantee. The accredited-repositories row is graded, not a uniform floor: ADS (Archaeology Data Service) enforces a domain metadata standard at ingest, so R1.3 scores 1 by construction, and is CoreTrustSeal-certified to 2027-02-12; DANS Data Station Archaeology enforces only a small generic mandatory core with the domain vocabulary block optional, so R1.3 requires rung-(i) evidence, and is certified to 2028-02-27; tDAR names no external standard, its preservation policy could not be verified, and its CoreTrustSeal certification lapsed on 2025-12-16 (renewal at First Submit), so R1.3 requires rung-(i) evidence. Certification status is a presence-floor credential informing by-construction weight, never a scoring input. The GitHub/GitLab row is split: GitHub licence detection may return NOASSERTION; GitLab exposes no SPDX identifier and reports licence data only on an opt-in query; neither platform carries a persistence entitlement. The publisher-supplement row: article-record persistence is a Crossref member obligation plus a retention right under "commercially reasonable efforts", not a warranty; F2, F3, F4, and A2 score 0 for supplement-only deposits; independently deposited supplements score as platform deposits, never under this row. The table is extensible by dated amendment or, pre-census, by gated instrument edit (Dataverse, Australian Data Archive, Figshare, and Dryad flagged). The adopted evaluation principle (registrant, 2026-08-15): platforms are evaluated once at the infrastructure layer — where every lodged artefact inherits the platform's characteristics — with artefact-level investigation reserved for facts the infrastructure leaves open; rows carry dated CoreTrustSeal and re3data evidence, and entitlements are always verified at the granting service, never taken from aggregators.

Fourth, the A1.2 no-restriction case: a fully open resource requiring no authentication satisfies A1.2 — the protocol supports authentication where needed, and none is needed; score 0 only where access control exists or is warranted but the mechanism is undocumented or unjustified. Fifth, F-block identifier granularity: F1 credits the artefact's own persistent identifier, or the article DOI where the artefact is distributed as that article's supplement — a declared departure from the strict object-PID reading (cf. F-UJI); F2, F3, and F4 remain strictly artefact-level, so supplement-only deposit yields F subtotals of 4/4 (own identifier), 1/4 (supplement under the article DOI), and 0/4 (unpublished). Sixth, R1.1 licence semantics: licences are assessed per artefact; the most-restrictive default applies only to disagreements about the same artefact's licence (publisher-hosted supplements default to the article's licence absent a separate one; paper-versus-service assertions resolve to the more restrictive); where the paper is silent, third-party-hosted artefacts score on the licence recorded at the service via the evidence pack, and a platform's mandatory licence field does not itself satisfy R1.1. Seventh, R1.3 qualifying standards: deposit-level standards only — generic metadata schemas, domain schemas and vocabularies, or deposit in an accredited domain repository whose ingest enforces its metadata standard (graded per the platform table); for code, package structure, CITATION.cff, CodeMeta, or community review; methodological standards (IntCal20, OxCal) do not qualify.

2. Evidence pack and read-scope re-specification (supersedes amendment 1, section 4)

Scoring spawns receive the paper source, the pushed instruments, and a per-paper verified evidence pack produced by a deterministic artefact-metadata harvester that resolves the paper's declared artefact links via enumerated endpoints (DataCite, Crossref, Zenodo, GitHub, GitLab, OSF; CRAN and Dryad flagged as early additions; extensible) into licence fields, metadata records, and conflict flags. Every pack record carries a retrieval timestamp and a response-content hash; packs are committed, receipt-covered artefacts whose hash is echoed in scoring receipts. Pack-staleness rule: packs are dated snapshots; the pack scored against is the committed pack whose hash the receipts echo; packs are re-harvested only between scoring cycles, never mid-run, so evidence is identical across the runs of a cycle; platform-state changes after a pack's harvest date are out of scope for that cycle's scores, and the harvest date is reported with results. Harvester-integrity commitment: endpoints that bot-gate scripted fetches (verified 2026-08-15: DANS returns HTTP 200 with a JavaScript challenge body — a silent false positive; tDAR returns 403) are fetched through a rendering step with challenge-page detection before any census reliance; where a register is served by a script-only application, its data source is queried directly and the queries recorded. Identifier-recovery rule (registrant, 2026-08-15): where a declared identifier fails to resolve — a cited DOI that 404s at its registrar, or a dead repository link — the harvest procedure attempts a bounded recovery before recording the gap: one search of the relevant registrar's public API (DataCite or Crossref) by title and author, and one search of the named repository where one is named. A recovered record enters the evidence pack flagged as recovered, with the query recorded, and is never silently substituted for the cited identifier: the citation defect remains visible and reportable as a study finding, while the recovered record supplies ordinary rung-(i) evidence about the artefact's actual status. Recovery is deliberately bounded — the analogue of the reproduction lane's minimal, documented code corrections — and a no-result after the two searches is recorded as a dead link, not pursued further (precedent case: marwick-2025's cited 10.5281/zenodo.14561925, resolving at neither DataCite nor Zenodo on 2026-08-15).

The isolation wording of amendment 1, section 4, is corrected to what is actually enforced (registrant's 2026-08-14 ruling: implement what can be implemented; amend the text where a control is infeasible). Spawn-time path-scoped read enforcement is infeasible in the execution harness's permission model, which is repository-wide; the operative controls are the agent tool allowlist (read-only tools); complete per-spawn file-access lists derived after the run from its transcript and archived with run artefacts; contamination flagging, under which any successful out-of-scope access fails the run; and a reconciliation gate that re-validates receipts from completed transcripts — including an attempts-are-not-reads rule, under which a declared read whose every attempt errored never entered context and is not a read — with a hard stop on failure. Empirical anchor: post-hoc reconciliation of all 45 retained benchmark transcripts found zero contaminating file accesses and machine-validated all receipts. The prohibition on reading persisted assessments stands unchanged.

3. Concordance reference re-derivation

The registered concordance check compared census-lane scores against pilot reference scores that are old-instrument and reproduction-informed — a two-axis mismatch that structurally caps concordance below the gate. The reference set is re-derived under the clarified instrument and the census input surface (paper plus evidence pack), with this pre-specified shape (registrant, 2026-08-15). Anchor: ruling-driven re-derivation with targeted human adjudication — starting from the registered reference set, the ratified clarifications are applied item by item; the registrant adjudicates every changed item, all 68 disputed items from the mining pass, and any item whose prior score rests on reproduction-only evidence (a census-surface check across all 150 items, scored through the evidence ladder); plus a spot-check sample of undisputed items. No machine-derived reference: a reference produced by the lane it gates would partly measure self-agreement. Blinding disclosure: the pilot adjudication is necessarily unblinded (the registrant has seen the benchmark outputs); the census human-validation subsample (registration section 8, n = 12) remains blinded and post-census exactly as registered, and both exercises run one shared hand-scoring protocol, which the pilot exercise rehearses. Registration of the new set: the re-derived scores are registered as a new reference dataset (E8 v2) with per-item provenance (prior score, ruling applied, adjudication note); the original set remains registered as historical; the concordance gate is re-pointed at the re-derived set. Entailed flips are documented before re-scoring (at minimum dye-et-al-2023 data R1.3; key-et-al-2024 A2; F-block items on both papers).

4. Re-validation design and remediation ladder

The reliability check re-runs on the clarified instrument, the v1.1 structured-output contract, and the reconciliation-gated harness, with the registered design otherwise unchanged: three arms by five pilot papers by three runs; stability and concordance against the re-derived reference, both gates at or above 0.90; model pins claude-sonnet-5, claude-opus-5, and claude-fable-5 unchanged; price-ordered selection unchanged. The below-threshold remediation ladder carries amendment 1, section 2's structure forward for the re-specified check: at most one routing-fix attempt (content delivery only, instrument text untouched) followed by a single re-run of the stability check; failing that, the registered majority-vote consequence applies with no further iteration, and the arm choice is recorded explicitly. The interpretation-guide promotion in section 5 is part of this amendment's pre-declared delivery specification, not a consumption of that routing-fix attempt.

5. Delivery and receipts specification

The FAIR-principles interpretation guide is promoted from pull to push: benchmark receipts showed inconsistent pulling across spawns (and attempt-based pull counts can mislead in both directions — one spawn's declared pulls all failed at a wrong path and it honestly scored guideless), so interpretive context is now uniform by construction. The guide is version-aligned to instrument v2.1, with an explicit supremacy clause: the instrument governs wherever the guide is silent or diverges. Every pushed instrument is registered with a content-integrity hash; the push layer refuses to inject text whose bytes differ from the registered hash, and receipts echo instrument versions and receipt tokens. Scoring outputs validate against structured-output contract v1.1: a self-identifying schema version, per-input non-scoring provenance, per-sub-principle evidence-pack citations, and escalation without fabricated scoring blocks.
